Ask five studios what a website costs and you get five different numbers, sometimes an order of magnitude apart. The reason is simple: the same word covers a one-page brochure and a booking platform, and those are not the same product being priced.

I build websites, and the question I get most often is some version of what this should cost. This article gives real Portuguese price ranges for 2026, explains what actually moves the number, and shows you how to read a quote so you can tell a fair one from a padded one.

## The price ranges you can expect

Published market prices in Portugal cluster into a few bands. Treat these as starting points, not guarantees:

- **One-page or landing site** — roughly €300 to €1,500. A single page with your offer, a contact form, and basic search setup. Good for a specific service or validating an idea.
- **Small business site (a few pages)** — roughly €800 to €2,500. Services, an about page, and contacts, designed around your brand.
- **Content site with a blog and SEO work** — roughly €1,500 to €6,000. Built to grow in Google over time, with a structure that can expand.
- **Custom build with integrations** — from around €2,500 upward, and often well past €15,000 for anything involving bookings, member areas, or connections to other systems.

The [published 2026 price guides](https://ideiascomsotaque.pt/blog/quanto-custa-um-website-em-portugal) from Portuguese studios land in the same territory, with the widest variation on the custom end. If a quote sits far outside these bands in either direction, that is a reason to ask more questions, not fewer.

## What actually moves the number

The page count is the least useful thing to price on, and it is what most people focus on. A five-page site can cost more than a fifteen-page one. What changes the figure is the work behind the pages.

**Integrations.** Anything the site has to talk to, such as a booking calendar, a payment provider, an invoicing tool, or a CRM, adds real engineering. Each connection has its own edge cases, and connecting to external systems in Portugal typically [runs into four figures on its own](https://netwods.com/quanto-custa-criar-um-site-em-portugal/) depending on how many systems and how messy their data is.

**Content and languages.** A site in Portuguese and English is not one site translated; it is two sets of copy that both have to read naturally and rank on their own. If you need the content written rather than supplied, that is a separate cost, and often the one that decides the timeline.

**Performance and design quality.** A template dropped onto a page is cheap. A site that loads fast on an ordinary phone, holds up against competitors, and turns visitors into enquiries takes deliberate work. That is the difference between a site that costs money and one that earns it. I wrote separately about how a [slow website quietly loses you revenue](/blog/slow-website-costing-you-money), and that gap is exactly what the cheaper end of the market skips.

## The costs nobody puts in the headline number

The build is a one-off. Running the site is not, and the recurring bill is where people get surprised a year later.

- **Domain** — a `.pt` or `.com` address costs roughly €10 to €25 a year.
- **Hosting** — anywhere from €60 to €300 a year for a normal business site, depending on traffic.
- **Maintenance** — updates, backups, security, and small changes. Professional plans commonly run [€30 to €150 a month](https://digitalxperience.pt/custo-criar-um-site-profissional-em-portugal/), though a simple site can go long stretches needing very little.

You do not always need a maintenance contract on day one. You do need to know it exists before you sign, so a €900 build does not turn into an argument six months in.

## Cheap, fast, good: you get two

Every quote is a position on a triangle of price, speed, and quality. A €400 site delivered in a week is real, and for the right business it is the correct choice. I have said before that for an unproven idea, a [builder or a small site is often the smart first move](/blog/hire-a-developer-vs-diy-your-website). What that price cannot also be is a custom-designed, integration-heavy site that carries your brand against serious competition.

When a quote looks too good, the saving is usually hiding in something: a generic template, no performance work, content you have to write yourself, or no plan for what happens after launch. None of those are dishonest on their own. They become a problem when nobody told you they were the trade-off.

## What a fair quote should tell you

Before comparing two numbers, make sure they describe the same thing. A proposal worth trusting says, in writing:

- **What is included** — page count, design work, who writes the content, and which integrations are in scope.
- **What you own at the end** — the code, the domain, and the accounts, in your name, not the developer's.
- **What happens after launch** — support, changes, and the cost of each.

When I built the site for [Dr Bárbara Barbizani](https://www.barbizanicarvalholaw.com/), a lawyer in Portugal, the budget covered a full brand refresh and a foundation for Google visibility, with no booking or online sales, so the number reflected that scope and nothing else. A quote that cannot tell you its own scope is not cheaper; it is just less clear, and unclear is where costs hide. If you are hiring, it helps to know [the warning signs to check before you sign](/blog/red-flags-before-you-hire-a-web-developer).

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a basic website cost in Portugal in 2026?**

A simple one-page or small business site typically runs from around €300 to €2,500, depending on design, content, and whether search setup is included. Recurring costs of roughly €100 to €400 a year for domain, hosting, and light maintenance sit on top of that.

**Why are website quotes so different from each other?**

Because the word covers everything from a single brochure page to a booking platform. Integrations, custom design, content writing, and performance work move the price far more than the number of pages does. Two quotes are only comparable when they list the same scope.

**Is it cheaper to build the website myself?**

For a presence site, usually yes: a builder can cost a few hundred euros a year. For a site meant to generate revenue, lower conversion and eventual migration costs often erase the saving within two to three years. I compared both paths in [hiring a developer versus doing it yourself](/blog/hire-a-developer-vs-diy-your-website).

**What ongoing costs should I budget for?**

Plan for the domain (€10–€25 a year), hosting (€60–€300 a year), and optional maintenance (€30–€150 a month). A site meant to grow in search also benefits from content work over time, which is a separate, ongoing investment rather than a one-off.
