Many health professionals with their own practice wonder whether they should adopt a booking system. The comparison is straightforward: how much time and money does managing the diary cost today, and how does that compare with the monthly fee for a scheduling platform?

I have built websites for health professionals whose day-to-day work differs considerably, and the answer changes with the practice, as it does with most engineering decisions. ## What the system does

Booking software displays available times and lets a patient reserve one without waiting for a phone call or reply. It records the appointment in the clinic's diary and sends a confirmation.

Reminders are usually part of the service. An SMS or email goes out a day or two before the consultation, using the contact details attached to the booking. Some platforms also let patients cancel or request a different time through the message.

Platforms available in Portugal, including [Buk](https://buk.pt/) and [EasyWeek](https://easyweek.pt/business/solutions/clinic), bundle these functions into a subscription. They reduce the routine work of confirming times, copying details into a diary and sending each reminder manually.

## When it pays

For two weeks, record the time spent answering requests, confirming appointments and handling changes. Include evenings. You can then compare those hours with the platform's complete monthly cost.

A practice with a few weekly consultations and a stable group of returning patients may manage well with a calendar and direct contact. The calculation changes when requests interrupt consultations or rescheduling takes up an evening. Recovering those hours can justify the fee.

Check the complete price. An advertised plan may limit the number of practitioners, charge separately for text messages or leave useful functions for a higher tier. Setup and support may cost extra. Ask for a quote based on the clinic's actual diaries and reminder volume, rather than relying on the entry price.

Missed appointments belong in the same calculation. An empty slot loses income and prevents another patient from using that time.

## The effect of reminders

[Mendov](https://www.mendov.com/) reports that SMS reminders and a round-the-clock online diary reduce missed or incorrectly scheduled appointments by about 20%. This is a vendor figure. The result in any clinic will depend partly on why patients miss consultations and whether their contact details are current.

Some marketing pages associate online scheduling with a 30–50% rise in bookings. [One industry source gives that range](https://wecondigital.com.br/como-aumentar-agendamentos-de-clinica-estrategias-comprovadas-para-multiplicar-suas-conversoes/), but it is a promotional claim rather than a reliable forecast. Demand, consultation prices and how easily patients find the practice also affect booking numbers.

Use your clinic's figures. With about 40 consultations a week and 4 no-shows, a 20% reduction would recover fewer than one consultation each week **[estimate — confirm]**. Multiply that result by the average consultation fee and compare it with the full software cost.

## The website's role

Context comes first. Patients usually look for the professional's field, approach, location and contact details before choosing a time. Sending people from a Google listing or advert directly to a booking calendar can leave all of those reasonable questions unanswered.

The website should provide enough context for a patient to proceed confidently. The appointment calendar can appear after the essential information instead of taking its place.

## Costs and failures

Test changes before launch. A cancellation should release the correct slot and notify the people involved. If two patients select the last opening at nearly the same time, the system must confirm it for only one of them.

Find out how the software reports an undelivered text message. Without an alert, staff may assume the reminder arrived and learn about the failure only after a missed consultation. Run these checks with test appointments before opening the system to patients.

Health appointment data is sensitive. Keep diagnoses and clinical details out of reminders, then check where the data is stored, who can access it and how it is deleted. The supplier's documentation or contract should answer those questions.

## How to decide

Start with two figures: diary-management hours and monthly no-shows. Then request the total cost for your actual volume of text messages, practitioners and clinic locations. When the likely saving covers that cost and makes everyday administration easier, test the service for a limited period.

Keep notes during the trial. Record booking changes, failed reminders and patients' questions. After a month, those records will tell you more than a sales promise; a low-volume practice can keep direct contact and repeat the calculation when its diary becomes busier.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a clinic booking system cost?

Most platforms charge a monthly subscription. The amount varies with the number of practitioners, diaries and functions, while text messages may cost extra. Ask for a price based on how the clinic will actually use the service.

### Do automated reminders actually reduce no-shows?

They can reduce no-shows caused by forgetfulness. One Portuguese vendor advertises a reduction of about 20% in missed and incorrectly scheduled appointments, though each practice should measure its own result.

### Does a small solo practice need online booking?

It depends. A steady diary with few requests may be managed directly, particularly when returning patients know how to get in touch. Software becomes more useful once replies, confirmations and rescheduling take up several hours.
